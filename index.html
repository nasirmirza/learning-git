The design in four sentences. A settlement instruction says where securities go for one account in one market; the business event says why they move. The platform stores the first as a directionless, effective-dated chain keyed on a DTCC country, security type and method combination, scoped to a client, fund or account. Nothing becomes usable without a second person approving it. At message time a resolver picks the most specific active instruction, and a builder renders it into MT540 to MT543 using the event's transaction type.

Part I: Domain background

1. Why settlement instructions matter here

Collateral businesses run on assets being in the right account at the right time. Firms carry continuous obligations: repo financing, securities lending, variation margin and initial margin on uncleared derivatives. A triparty agent such as BNY takes over the operational half of those obligations, but it can only allocate what is already in its world. Every movement across that boundary, into a client's inventory or back out, is a settlement instruction, and every instruction depends on a correct standing settlement instruction (SSI) for that account and market.

Most settlement fails trace back to reference data rather than messaging: a wrong participant number, a stale agent account, a missing market identifier. An SSI platform therefore earns its keep through three things: it only allows combinations the market supports, it forces a second pair of eyes on every change, and it resolves the same answer every time for the same question.

3. Accounts and the two settlement layers

  • No client accounts at the depository. Segregation is contractual and operational, enforced on BNY's books and reconciled daily against the omnibus position.
  • Accounts are per legal entity and per platform. A manager with 300 funds has 300 longboxes, and roughly one segregated account per fund per receiver. A fund on both Domestic and Global platforms has two longboxes.
  • The custody to triparty hop. BNY custody and BNY triparty can hold different participant numbers at the same depository, for legal capacity, risk controls and reconciliation reasons. When they do, a move between a client's custody account and its longbox is a real depository settlement, instructed with MT542 and MT540, even though both accounts are at BNY. When both sit under the same participant account, it is an internal book entry. The data model records this explicitly (section 11).

The two instruction layers

Without the MT540, an incoming asset lands in suspense at BNY and cannot be allocated, even though it is legally the client's. Whether BNY also inputs a matching instruction depends on the market: matching markets (ICSDs, T2S, CREST) need both sides; Fedwire is unilateral; DTC deliveries pass BNY's Receiver Authorized Delivery checks.

Transaction type is a role, not a direction

Field :22F::SETR// states the business reason for the movement and the party's role in it. Direction comes from the message type. The ISO usage rules show the same code on the opening and closing legs:

Consequence for the design: the transaction type belongs to the business event, not to the SSI. The SSI may carry only an account-transfer default (OWNI or OWNE). A TRAD code on a free transfer between a fund's own accounts, or COLI/COLO on an against-payment instruction, should be rejected before a message is built. In most markets SETR is not a matching field, so a wrong code settles silently.

5. Free of payment and against payment

Margin and inventory moves are free of payment (MT540/542): nobody pays for collateral. Against-payment instructions (MT541/543) appear where cash really changes hands: outright purchases and sales on longbox inventory, bilateral or sponsored DVP repo, securities lending against cash collateral, dealer Treasury clearance and cross-border bond trades. Triparty repo is economically DVP but settles both legs on BNY's books, with no MT541/543.

DvP modelSecuritiesCashExamples
1Gross, real timeGross, real timeFedwire Securities, T2S, Euroclear Bank
2Gross, through the dayNet, end of dayDTC
3NetNetCCP netting, e.g. NSCC

What changes in MT541/543: an :19A::SETT amount is mandatory; matching markets compare currency and amount within a tolerance (T2S: €25 above €100,000, €2 below); cash parties are needed only for split cash settlement; and confirmations are MT545/547. For the SSI platform this means a DVP-specific SSI is needed only when the cash leg differs from the default, and currency joins the resolution key for those rows.

7. Clients, funds and who instructs

  • The fund is the principal. It owns the assets, has its own LEI and signs its own ISDA, CSA, GMRA or MRA. Sub-funds of an umbrella are ring-fenced, so their assets and longboxes never pool.
  • The manager is the instructing party. It sends messages from its own BIC under a mandate; the fund is identified by account number in :97A::SAFE. The manager holds no asset accounts at BNY for client assets, only a relationship layer: connectivity, users, fees and a reporting hierarchy for viewing, not pooling.
  • The fund appoints the custodian legally (board, trustee or management company); the manager usually runs the selection. For segregated mandates the asset owner dictates the custodian.
  • SWIFT is optional for the client. Every principal needs an LEI and an account. Connectivity can be direct SWIFT, a service bureau, file transfer, the NEXEN portal or a third party instructing under mandate. The platform must not assume the account owner has a BIC.
  • Securities lending makes a fund a receiver. A loan is title transfer; the borrower posts collateral to the lending fund, which sets the eligibility schedule and monitors but instructs nothing on the way in.

8. SSI standards: ALERT and the DTCC market rules

DTCC ALERT stores accounts and SSIs keyed by country of settlement, security type and method (the final depository), organised under settlement models and attached to an account access code. Custodians can own and maintain the golden copy through Global Custodian Direct. ALERT is shaped around trades; BNY's triparty receipt instructions come from BNY's own onboarding pack, not from ALERT.

DTCC's Market Business Rules for Investment Managers (publication AL996, 29 October 2025) lists every valid combination and what each needs. Analysis of that file:

Part II: Application design

9. Goals, scope and users

Goals

  • Only market-valid instructions can exist.
  • Every change approved by a second person.
  • Deterministic resolution: same question, same answer, explainable.
  • Full history: what was valid on any date, and who changed it.
  • Vendor neutral: serves custodians, agents and asset managers alike.

Out of scope for version 1

  • Collateral allocation and eligibility (triparty agent's job).
  • Trade capture and matching.
  • FX and cash payment SSIs (planned extension).
  • Direct SWIFT connectivity; the platform hands rendered messages to an existing gateway.
RoleCan do
MakerCreate, edit and retire SSIs, accounts and participant accounts as change requests; run bulk imports
CheckerApprove or reject other people's change requests; never their own
Reference data adminLoad DTCC catalogue editions, codify field rules, enable markets and set defaults per tenant (also maker-checker)
ViewerSearch, view history, run resolution tests
Service accountCall the resolver and message builder from settlement systems

10. Architecture

  • One service, clear modules. Version 1 is a single deployable service with modular boundaries; split later only if load demands it.
  • Database enforces the invariants that matter most (valid combination, no overlap, four eyes, tenant isolation), so a bug in the application layer cannot corrupt the data.
  • Read path is hot, write path is rare. The resolver is called per settlement instruction and must be fast and cached by key and business date; changes are a handful per day per tenant.

12. Reference data management

Reference data has three layers, and the portal reads only the top one:

  1. DTCC catalogue: countries, security types, methods, combinations and field rules, loaded from each DTCC edition into staging, compared with the current one, then published. The build script is in tools/build_reference_data.py.
  2. Codified rules: your team turns DTCC prose into condition_expr and validation_regex, starting with the markets you use. DTCC text is kept verbatim beside it.
  3. Tenant layer: which combinations each tenant offers (for example only markets BNY's triparty platform supports) and the default method for each of the 71 pairs with a choice. DTCC publishes no defaults in this file.
Catalogue changeEffect

13. Portal screens and behaviour

SSI setup: a cascade, then a generated form

  1. Scope: client, fund or account, chosen first so the screen can show what this SSI would override and what would override it.
  2. Country: searchable by name or ISO code, restricted to combinations the tenant has enabled.
  3. Security type: filtered to the country; standard types first, alternates labelled.
  4. Method: auto-filled and locked when only one exists. When there is a choice, show each option with its variant (T2S, local regime, alternate type, Stock Connect, Bond Connect), preselect the tenant default and show DTCC's guidance beside it.
  5. Chain and identifiers: rendered from ref_field_rule. Not-applicable fields are hidden; mandatory fields block submission; recommended fields warn; conditional fields are evaluated live as the user types; format hints come from DTCC's wording.
  6. Validity and options: valid from, valid to, permitted direction, payment type and cash leg only when DVP is chosen.
  7. Submit creates a change request, not an SSI.

The UI reads reference-data/ssi_market_cascade.json (or the equivalent API) so the cascade works without a round trip per selection.

14. Workflows

Maker-checker

  • The checker sees a field-level diff, validation results and the resolution impact ("this will replace the SSI used by 14 accounts").
  • The database refuses approval by the maker, and refuses an approval that would create overlapping active versions.
  • Edits never change an active version; they create a new version with a later start date. Rejection leaves the current version untouched.
  • Retiring an SSI is also a change request, setting valid_to.

Bulk import

  1. Upload templates/SSI_import_template_v2.xlsx; reject the file if any is_sample = YES rows remain.
  2. Validate every row: combination exists and is enabled, scope references exist, field rules satisfied, dates valid, no two rows share a key and overlapping dates, no clash with live SSIs.
  3. If any row fails, reject the whole file with a row-level report. Partial loads leave the data in a half-migrated state that is hard to reason about.
  4. On success, raise one change request per row under a shared batch id; checkers can approve per row or per batch, still under four eyes.

Catalogue refresh

  1. Load the new DTCC edition into staging with literal codes preserved (Namibia is "NA").
  2. Diff against the published catalogue: added, withdrawn and changed combinations and rules.
  3. Produce the impact report on live SSIs; reference data admin reviews.
  4. Publish under maker-checker; withdrawn rows stay for history.

15. Resolving an SSI

Inputs: tenant, account, country, security type, method, payment type, currency, direction, business date. Output: one SSI version, or a clear error.

  1. Find the account's fund and client.
  2. Walk the security type up its hierarchy (EQA to EQU) so a broader SSI can serve a narrower request.
  3. Candidates: active versions valid on the business date, matching country and method, payment type equal or ANY, currency equal or blank, scope matching the account, fund or client, and permitted for the direction.
  4. Score: account 300, fund 200, client 100; minus 10 per security-type level walked; plus 5 for exact payment type; plus 2 for exact currency.
  5. Return the top candidate. If the top two tie, raise an ambiguity error. Never guess: silent fallback is how an asset lands at the wrong depository.

Implementation: ssi_pkg.resolve in db/03_ssi_pkg.sql. Cache results by key and business date; invalidate on approval.

When no method is supplied (a trade arrives with only an ISIN), derive country and security type from instrument data, then use the tenant default method for that pair. Treat a missing default as an error, not a reason to pick one.

16. Generating settlement messages

From chain role to MT tag

A movement needs two SSIs: the instructing account's own SSI (for its safekeeping account) and the counterparty side's SSI (rendered as the other side). The counterparty chain becomes the receiving side in a delivery and the delivering side in a receipt:

Chain roleIn MT542/543 (receiving side)In MT540/541 (delivering side)
Depository (from method):95P::PSET:95P::PSET
LOCAL_AGENT:95P::REAG or :95R::REAG/scheme/, with its SAFE:95P::DEAG or :95R::DEAG
INTERMEDIARY:95P::RECU:95P::DECU
GLOBAL_CUSTODIAN:95P::BUYR:95P::SELL
ACCOUNT_OWNER:97A::SAFE in the BUYR block:97A::SAFE in the SELL block
Own account:97A::SAFE in sequence C

Market identifiers map per field rule: at DTC the participant number becomes :95R::REAG/DTCYID/; at Fedwire the ABA becomes :95R::REAG/USFW/. Placements other than these are recorded in ref_field_rule.mt54x_placement as suggestions to confirm against BNY's message specification.

Transaction type and payment

  • SETR from event_setr_mapping by event type, party role and leg; for account transfers, the SSI default (OWNI or OWNE) applies.
  • Message type from direction and payment type: FOP to MT540/542, DVP to MT541/543 with mandatory :19A::SETT.
  • Partial settlement, beneficial ownership and repo indicators in :22F:: come from the event, not the SSI.

Worked example: custody to triparty at DTC

Account CUST-88421 (BNY custody participant) moves corporate bonds to LONGBOX-002 (BNY triparty participant). The accounts sit under different DTC participant numbers, so transfer_route marks it as a depository settlement, event ACCOUNT_TRANSFER_INTERNAL, SETR OWNI. The builder resolves both SSIs for US-COB-DTC and emits:

MT542 to custody                       MT540 to triparty
:97A::SAFE//CUST-88421                 :97A::SAFE//LONGBOX-002
:22F::SETR//OWNI                       :22F::SETR//OWNI
:95R::REAG/DTCYID/<triparty part #>   :95R::DEAG/DTCYID/<custody part #>
:95P::PSET//DTCYUS33                   :95P::PSET//DTCYUS33

If both accounts resolved to the same participant account, the builder would instead emit an internal transfer with no depository leg.

ISO 20022

The same resolved chain renders to sese.023 through a second role mapping; the stored data does not change.

18. Security, tenancy and audit

  • Tenant isolation in the database with Oracle Virtual Private Database: the service sets the tenant in an application context per request, and a policy predicate filters every table. A portal bug cannot cross tenants.
  • Single write path: the application has EXECUTE on ssi_pkg and no DML grants on tables.
  • History: effective dating answers "what was valid on date X"; Oracle Flashback Data Archive answers "what did the table look like at time T" with no trigger code; change_request records intent and reasons.
  • Authentication: firm SSO; roles mapped from directory groups; service accounts for system callers with resolve-only rights.
  • Sensitive data: account numbers and investor IDs are confidential reference data; encrypt at rest and mask in logs.

21. Open items and assumptions

  • Confirm with BNY: entity BICs and participant numbers per market and business line; expected SETR codes per flow; whether custody and triparty share participant accounts in each market; cut-offs and allocation cycle times.
  • Interpretations to verify: security type descriptions beyond EQU, COB, TRY and MMT; NSD, which DTCC does not define in this file; the chain-role and MT placement columns in the field rules.
  • Defaults: operations must choose a tenant default for each of the 71 country and security type pairs with several methods.
  • Codifying conditions: 2,242 conditional rules are prose; prioritise the markets in use.
  • DTCC content: the reference data derives from a DTCC publication marked all rights reserved and includes guidance text; keep this repository private or remove the verbatim text before sharing outside the firm.
  • Untested code: the SQL parses with an Oracle-dialect parser (the virtual column keyword aside) but has not been compiled on a database; the package needs unit tests before use.
  • Assumed platform: Oracle 19c or later; firm SSO; an existing SWIFT gateway.
